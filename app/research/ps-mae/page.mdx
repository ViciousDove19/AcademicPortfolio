# PS-MAE

<Stamp>Under review, IEEE JBHI</Stamp>

*A clinically-informed peritumoral masked autoencoder for axillary lymph
node status assessment from breast ultrasound.*

## In plain language

Here's the clinical fact this whole project starts from: whether breast
cancer has spread to the axillary lymph nodes isn't only written in the
tumour itself — margin irregularity and changes in the tissue right around
it are part of how radiologists read that risk. Most imaging models never
get the chance to learn that, because they're pretrained by masking random
patches anywhere in the image and reconstructing raw pixels, which spends
most of their capacity on ultrasound speckle rather than anything
diagnostic.

PS-MAE changes two things at once. It masks a ring of tissue drawn *only*
around the tumour — not the lesion, not random patches — and instead of
asking the model to redraw pixels, it asks the model to reconstruct the
*edges* in that ring. Edges survive speckle noise; raw pixels don't. On
their own, neither change does much. Together, they do.

## Overview

Axillary lymph node metastasis (ALNM) status is a critical factor in
breast-cancer treatment planning, but confirming it currently requires an
invasive biopsy. Predicting it from ultrasound instead is attractive but
hard: labelled data is scarce, and ultrasound's low signal-to-noise ratio
means most self-supervised pretraining wastes capacity on noise rather than
the peritumoral tissue that actually carries the prognostic signal.

## Method

A U-Net first segments the tumour-like region on a large pool of unlabeled
breast-ultrasound images, and the boundary is dilated outward by a few
patches to define a peritumoral ring. PS-MAE masks only that ring during
pretraining — not random patches — and reconstructs the Sobel edge-gradient
response of the masked region rather than raw pixel values, which
emphasises boundary and texture structure over speckle. The encoder itself
starts from ultrasound-domain weights (USFM) rather than ImageNet, so it
isn't spending early training unlearning natural-image statistics. Once
pretraining is done, the decoder is discarded and the frozen encoder is
evaluated with a linear probe on labelled ALNM data — with attention
rollout used afterward to check how much of the model's attention actually
lands inside the peritumoral ring it was trained on.

## Data

Pretraining pooled 83,271 unlabeled frames from 11 public breast-ultrasound
sources. Downstream evaluation used an internal cohort of 698 images from
175 patients, and a fully held-out external cohort from AIIMS New Delhi —
88 patients, 176 images — that never appeared in pretraining.

## Results

On the internal cohort, PS-MAE leads a linear-probe AUROC comparison
against seven baselines, including two generic self-supervised models
(DINOv2, ImageNet-pretrained) and two ultrasound-domain ones (USFM,
USF-MAE):

## Status & links

First-author manuscript under review at IEEE Journal of Biomedical and
Health Informatics. Code and weights will be released on publication.
Validated so far at a single external institution — broader multi-centre
validation and adaptive (rather than fixed-width) ring masking are the
identified next steps.
