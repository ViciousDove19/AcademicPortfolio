# WAAU-Net

<Stamp>Under review, IEEE JBHI</Stamp>

*Wavelet-domain adversarial training for cross-population segmentation.*

## In plain language

A segmentation model trained on ultrasound from one population often gets
worse when it's applied to scans from another — sometimes because the
disease presents differently, but often just because the scanners and
acquisition settings differ. WAAU-Net tries to separate those two effects
and correct for the acquisition-driven part by aligning the frequency bands,
via wavelets, where those differences concentrate.

## Overview

Breast ultrasound segmentation models degrade when moved between scanner
populations. WAAU-Net applies adversarial alignment in the wavelet domain,
targeting the frequency bands where acquisition differences concentrate, to
improve robustness across populations.

## Method

An adaptive wavelet normalization module is combined with adversarial
alignment in the wavelet domain, so the model is trained to be invariant to
the frequency bands where acquisition differences concentrate rather than
to the full image spectrum.

## Data

Evaluated across demographically distinct cohorts — Indian, Polish, and
Egyptian — to separate segmentation failures caused by acquisition
differences from those caused by genuine biological variation.

## Results

Shows that segmentation performance degrades across these cohorts for both
acquisition and biological reasons, and that the adaptive wavelet
normalization module narrows that gap. Full quantitative results are
pending publication.
