# WAAU-Net

<Stamp>Preprint, submitted to BSPC</Stamp>

*Wavelet-domain adversarial adaptation for cross-cohort generalization in
breast ultrasound tumor segmentation.*

## In plain language

Before building anything, this project asks a diagnostic question first:
when a segmentation model trained on one hospital's scans fails on
another's, *where does that failure actually live*? A radiomics analysis —
650 features, checked for which ones differ significantly between three
hospital cohorts — found that most of the divergence wasn't scattered
evenly across the image. It concentrated in the wavelet domain: 60% of the
features that differed significantly between cohorts came from
wavelet-decomposed versions of the image, not the raw pixels.

So WAAU-Net doesn't try to scrub "domain information" out of the whole
image at once, the way most adversarial domain-adaptation methods do.
It decomposes the image into frequency bands first, and only normalises
the bands the data pointed to.

## Overview

Breast ultrasound segmentation models are usually trained and tested within
a single hospital cohort, and lose accuracy when moved to another — cohorts
differ simultaneously in scanner, acquisition protocol, and patient
population, and that shift tends to hit underrepresented groups hardest.
Existing domain-adaptation methods work in the spatial domain and suppress
"domain information" as one undifferentiated whole, with no way to tell
whether diagnostically useful structure got suppressed along with it.

## Method

Each image is passed through a discrete wavelet transform, producing four
subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH). An Adaptive Wavelet Normalization (AWN) module
learns, per subband, a gate that blends the raw signal with an
instance-normalised version of it — a high gate keeps the raw signal, a low
gate replaces it with the normalised form. The whole network — an Attention
U-Net backbone reading the gated subbands — is trained jointly with a
gradient-reversed discriminator that tries to guess which hospital an image
came from; the encoder is pushed to make that guess fail. The intuition
mirrors the radiomics finding: tumour localisation mostly lives in LL and
LH (intensity contrast and true tissue edges), while HL and HH carry more
of the cohort-varying texture and acquisition artifact — so selectively
normalising only the high-frequency bands should strip cohort-specific
noise without erasing the diagnostic signal.

## Data

Three cohorts, restricted to malignant cases and balanced to 98 each:
AIIMS New Delhi (410 images, 119 patients), the open-access BUSI cohort
from Egypt (sampled to 98 from 153 eligible malignant images), and the
open-access BrEaST cohort from Poland (98 of 256 patients). Evaluated with
5-fold cross-validation internally, and leave-one-cohort-out for external,
never-seen-cohort testing.

## Results

Internally, WAAU-Net is competitive with the strongest baseline (Dice 0.73
vs. nnU-Net's 0.74) — the real difference shows up externally, on the
held-out BrEaST cohort, where WAAU-Net holds the smallest internal→external
gap (0.090) of any model tested:

## Status & links

Preprint submitted to *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*, not yet
peer-reviewed. Code is available at
[github.com/ViciousDove19/WAAUNet](https://github.com/ViciousDove19/WAAUNet).
By design, this study can't separate scanner, population, and protocol
effects from one another, and the cohorts are small and malignant-only —
broader, subgroup-level validation is the identified next step.
